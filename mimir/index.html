Your world is bigger than a pile of notes.

Complex worlds should become clearer as they grow.

Pages are useful, but a world is made from the relationships between them. Mimir connects characters, places, cultures, factions, events and stories so you can move from a detail to the wider world around it.

See the same canon through trees, timelines, maps, backlinks and relationships. Build worlds, not documents.

Mimir AI

Ask your world. Get an answer grounded in your world.

Mimir is not there to make up your canon for you. It retrieves relevant lore, answers from material you have actually written and should make uncertainty visible rather than filling gaps with confident invention.

Question: Who killed King Aldric?

Mimir: There is no confirmed killer in your current canon. Three entries implicate House Vael, but none state that they carried out the assassination.

This example explains the intended behaviour. It is not presented as a product screenshot.

Lore Consistency Review

Find contradictions before your audience does.

Run a review across the canon to surface conflicting dates, incompatible relationships, duplicate facts, broken references and other issues that become difficult to spot as a world grows.

Proving Grounds

Don't just document your world. Test it.

Prototype conversations, quest logic, tabletop sessions, board-game ideas and other interactive scenarios before they become canon. Use the world you have already built as the context for testing what happens next.

Collaboration and hosting

Build together without making everyone a power user.

Writers can share a collaborative workspace. Game Masters can host the world while players join as viewers or controlled participants using Mimir Core. Permissions decide what each person can see and change.

Mimir is not a public social network or a place to meet strangers. It is built for work with the collaborators, friends and players you already know.

Local-first by design

Your world belongs to you.

Your core world lives locally in readable files. You can use the local foundation offline. Cancelling or downgrading does not erase the world you created or lock you out of your own files.

  • Unlimited local worlds and entries across every tier.
  • Migration and export are not premium privileges.
  • Local work survives a downgrade.

Network functionality such as Mimir AI, hosted collaboration and hosted worlds necessarily transmits the selected data needed to provide that feature. Production subprocessors and retention details will be published once verified.
